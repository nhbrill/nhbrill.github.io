I recently had the opportunity to attend Splunk .conf26, where I gained valuable insight into the capabilities of the Splunk platform and further developed my knowledge of SPL (Splunk's Processing Language).

{/* truncate */}
A central theme throughout the conference was "Trusted AI at Scale." Cisco demonstrated how Splunk can be leveraged to monitor AI agent activity, analyze agent logs, and help ensure that confidential or sensitive information is not inadvertently exposed. The platform also provides visibility into agent behavior, helping organizations verify that AI systems are operating as intended. This is an important capability as we continue to develop and adopt AI-powered tools and automation.
<br ></br><br ></br>
One of the key takeaways for me was the growing importance of automation in cybersecurity. As attackers increasingly adopt AI, security operations can no longer rely solely on human-speed detection and response. Solutions like Splunk can help identify suspicious activity, detect potential security incidents within application logs, and accelerate response efforts before issues become larger threats.
<br ></br><br ></br>
In my day-to-day work, I have been using Splunk to monitor an enterprise portal by:
- Tracking HTTP response status codes to identify and troubleshoot errors, allowing me to proactively resolve issues before they result in tickets.
- Monitoring query endpoints for timeouts and performance issues, helping identify and address reliability concerns before they impact end users.
- Visualizing portal usage and activity trends through dashboards and reports, enabling analysis of peak usage periods, user behavior, and business KPIs to better understand how the portal is being utilized.
